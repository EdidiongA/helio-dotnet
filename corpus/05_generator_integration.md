# Running a generator with a hybrid system

A generator connected to a hybrid inverter's AC input is treated as a grid source. The inverter will synchronise to it, pass it through to the loads, and use the surplus to charge the battery. Most hybrid inverters need a generator at least 1.5 times the inverter's rated charge power to synchronise reliably; a 5 kVA inverter charging at 3 kW wants a generator of 4.5 kVA or more.

Small petrol generators without automatic voltage regulation produce unstable frequency and voltage that hybrid inverters reject. Symptoms are repeated connect and disconnect cycles and an AC input out of range alarm. Enabling the inverter's generator mode widens the accepted voltage and frequency window; if the generator still does not hold, it is not suitable as an input source.

Limit the battery charge current from the generator to about 60 to 70 percent of the generator's continuous rating so that household loads do not push it into overload. Running a generator only to charge the battery is wasteful: a 2.5 kVA petrol set burns about 1 litre per hour to deliver about 1.5 kWh into the battery after losses.

Never connect a generator and the utility grid to the same inverter input at the same time. Use a manual or automatic changeover switch with a break-before-make action so the two sources can never be paralleled.
