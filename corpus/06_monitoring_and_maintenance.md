# Monitoring, maintenance and diagnosing low yield

Daily yield in kWh is the first number to track. A 1.8 kW array in Lagos should produce between 5 and 8 kWh on a clear dry-season day and 3 to 5 kWh in the rainy season. Compare each day's figure with the same month of the previous year; a drop of more than 15 percent with similar weather indicates a problem.

If yield drops suddenly, suspect soiling or a failed string first. Bird droppings or a single shaded cell can cut a whole string's output because bypass diodes only isolate part of a panel. Wash panels with plain water and a soft brush in the early morning, never with cold water on a hot panel, which can crack the glass.

A gradual decline over months points to degradation of connectors or cables. Check the MC4 connectors for discolouration, test each string's open-circuit voltage against the others under the same light, and measure the operating current with a clamp meter. One string reading 20 percent below its neighbours is faulty.

Battery capacity should be checked every six months by logging a full discharge from 100 percent to the cutoff under a known load. A lithium iron phosphate bank that delivers less than 80 percent of its rated amp-hours has reached end of life for backup duty.

Inverter fans draw dust in; clean the heat sink and fan filters quarterly. An inverter that derates on hot afternoons is almost always a cooling problem, not an electrical one.
