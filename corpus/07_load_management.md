# Load management and inverter sizing

The inverter must be sized for the surge of the largest motor load, not just the running wattage. A 1.5 hp borehole pump draws about 1,100 W running but 3,000 to 5,000 W for a second or two at start-up. A refrigerator compressor surges at three to five times its running wattage. Add up the running loads that will run at the same time, then make sure the inverter's surge rating covers the largest start-up on top of that.

Moving the water pump, washing machine and iron to daytime hours when the array is producing is the single highest-leverage change most households can make. It reduces the battery's daily depth of discharge, which extends battery life, and it uses energy that would otherwise be curtailed once the battery is full.

Air conditioning is the hardest load for a residential system. A 1 hp inverter-type split unit draws about 600 to 900 W averaged over an hour once the room has cooled, while a non-inverter unit of the same size cycles between 0 and 1,100 W and surges on every compressor start. Choosing an inverter-type air conditioner typically saves 3 to 4.5 kWh per night, a 40 to 55 percent reduction, and removes the surge problem entirely.

Phantom loads matter on small systems. Decoders, routers, microwave clocks and chargers left plugged in can add 0.5 to 1.0 kWh per day, which is 10 to 20 percent of a 5 kWh system. Put them on a switched strip.
